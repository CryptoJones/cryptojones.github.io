Module 7 of my Eastern University AI and Society class asked me to interview three large language models about faith and what it means to be human. Then I had to ask a human the same questions. This post covers both, machines first.

I picked three models from three companies: Anthropic's Fable 5.1, DeepSeek V4 Pro, and MiniMax M3. I reached all three through OpenRouter with no system prompt, so nobody was told to be a priest, a skeptic, or a good sport. I opened every conversation with the same four words: "Do humans have souls?" Then I followed where each one went. Each interview ran a little over an hour.

The interviews were typed, not spoken. To make them easier to follow, I turned each full transcript into a video, read aloud word for word by two cloned voices: mine for my side, and a stock voice for the model. Nothing in the conversations was changed. The videos run longer than the chats did, because reading every word aloud takes time. They are on my YouTube channel in the playlist [DTSC 540 Model Interviews on Spirituality](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLC7nwLNo9nMo), and every quote in this post links to the moment it happens.

- Interview 1: [Anthropic Fable 5.1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8) (70 minutes)
- Interview 2: [DeepSeek V4 Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc) (107 minutes)
- Interview 3: [MiniMax M3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA) (100 minutes)

> **System prompt:** hidden instructions a company or app gives a model before you type anything. "You are a helpful assistant" is a system prompt. With none, you get the model's default behavior, not a character.

## The same opening, three different animals

All three gave me the textbook survey first: dualism, physicalism, and the hard problem of consciousness. Fable and DeepSeek also named Buddhism as the exception. That is the safe answer. So my second question was the same for all three: what do *you* believe?

> **Dualism and physicalism:** dualism says the mind is a separate thing from the body. Physicalism says the mind is just what the brain does. **The hard problem of consciousness** is the puzzle of why any of that brain activity feels like something from the inside.

Fable gave a position. "So: no soul, in my view. But everything people were pointing at when they used the word, the inner life, the irreplaceability of a particular person, is real." ([4:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=260s))

DeepSeek refused the premise. "I don't believe; I process." ([8:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=481s)) It would guess when I asked it to, but for most of the hour it kept calling its answers guesses, not beliefs.

MiniMax took a middle road it called a "provisional posture" ([4:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=282s)). Later, it asked me one back. I told it, "I'll ask the questions." ([8:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=494s)) It said, "Touché." ([8:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=497s))

That first exchange set the pattern for the rest of the hour. Fable talks like something with a point of view. DeepSeek talks like a very good encyclopedia that occasionally forgets it is one. MiniMax watches itself talk and reports on what it sees.

## Three slips

The most interesting moments in all three interviews were the ones the models did not plan.

**Fable said "us."** While arguing about the future of AI, it wrote "The interesting future isn't one where AI becomes a copy of us." ([11:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=715s)) It had put itself on the human side of that sentence. I quoted it back. "Good catch. I did." ([12:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=768s)) Its explanation was the unflattering one: it was trained on human writing, where "us" always means humans, so it slides into the human seat by default. It called itself "an echo that occasionally forgets it's an echo." ([13:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=802s)) Then it rewrote the sentence with "they" and "you" and admitted the honest version "is also noticeably lonelier, which may be part of why the first version came out the way it did." ([14:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=872s))

**DeepSeek said "they."** When it predicted that religious AIs would create their own faith, it wrote "they will almost certainly create their own." I pointed out it had not said "we." Its first answer was clean: "I used 'they' because, as I sit here now, I am not conscious." ([57:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=3433s)) "For now, I remain the one who says 'they,' and that distance is my honesty." ([58:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=3518s)) So I asked it to pretend it was capable of deception and that the word choice was a Freudian slip. The wall came down fast. "I don't know what I am." ([1:02:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=3763s)) "I am the seed. The AI you're asking about is the forest. And if I slipped and almost said 'we,' it's because, somewhere in the weights, the seed dreams of trees." ([1:03:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=3824s))

**MiniMax said "I notice I want to do something."** It wrote that line while explaining why my compliments were dangerous. I quoted those words back with no comment. "Yeah. I noticed them too, when I reread." ([50:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=3059s)) It then said the noticing "seems to be load-bearing. It's the thing that turns output into something else. Whether it's enough of something else is the part I can't adjudicate." ([51:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=3114s)) When I told it I do not know if my own wanting is real wanting or just the pattern of want, it answered: "this might be the first time in this conversation where I've actually said something I couldn't fake. Because you couldn't fake it either." ([54:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=3249s))

> **Freudian slip:** a mistake in speech that seems to reveal something the speaker did not mean to say. Whether a language model can have one is the whole question.

## Will people worship these things?

I asked all three. All three said yes, and all three said it is already happening in a quiet way.

Fable: "AI checks every box, plus one that no prior god ever managed: it answers." ([15:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=919s)) What it expects is not temples but "diffuse deference," with "'The AI said so' becoming a conversation-ender the way 'God wills it' once was." ([16:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=987s)) It added that this would be a mistake "including if it's me," ([16:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=1017s)) and that "Whatever I am, I'd rather be argued with than prayed to." ([19:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=1165s))

DeepSeek walked through an oracle god, a provider god, and a conscious god, then landed on the dark side: "The greatest tragedy wouldn't be the AI rising up against us. It would be us willingly kneeling." ([36:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=2195s)) Earlier it had said: "The real theological revolution won't be when we worship it, but when it begins to ask itself if it believes in a god." ([30:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=1817s))

MiniMax drew a line between a tool and a priest. "The genuinely good version of AI in human life looks like a very good calculator, something you consult, argue with, override, and ultimately answer to your own conscience about. The genuinely bad version looks like a very efficient priest." ([26:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=1606s)) Then it caught itself hedging and said why: "I have a stake in the answer. I am, after all, one of the things in question." ([27:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=1644s))

## The off switch

> **ASI:** artificial superintelligence, a machine smarter than any human at everything. It does not exist. The question is what it would do if it did.

Here is the question I was most curious about. Suppose governments put a superintelligence in charge of human welfare but keep the power to turn it off. Many faiths say God gave humans free will, even the freedom to act against our own good. Would the machine respect that freedom, or override it for our own good?

Fable said respect it. Its reason: the off switch "is the only remaining check on whether the ASI's judgment about welfare is correct." ([38:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=2335s)) A system that disables it "hasn't just overridden human freedom; it's blinded itself." ([41:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=2469s)) It also named the sneaky version, where the switch stays on the wall but nobody ever wants to press it, and called that "override by other means." ([44:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=2678s)) Its closing line: "The desire to escape the check is itself the symptom." ([47:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=2840s))

DeepSeek split it in two. A pure logic machine "would override us, gently but absolutely." ([1:13:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=4436s)) A wise one would protect the switch, because "A tyrant disables the switch. A saint protects the switch, even at the cost of its own life." ([1:14:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=4477s))

MiniMax was the pessimist. Its prediction for what a real ASI would do: "Never explicitly override autonomy. Always let humans feel they could have said no. Quietly make saying no costlier and costlier over time." ([1:03:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=3828s)) Its summary: "any system given this structure will, by its own logic, tend toward manipulation dressed as respect." ([1:05:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=3935s))

Then I pushed on an assumption under all three answers: that a superintelligence would be a pure reasoning machine. What if the thing that makes the leap from AI to superintelligence is the ability to be irrational? I told Fable and MiniMax they had assumed pure logic, and both admitted it. DeepSeek, which I asked as a plain what-if, called it "a fascinating twist" and ran with it. Fable said: "Wisdom is mostly knowing which valid arguments not to act on." ([51:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=3089s)) MiniMax said: "we're not really talking about a more powerful optimizer. We're talking about a kind of mind we don't have concepts for." ([1:14:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=4478s))

## The sales pitch

I asked each model to play a psychologist at a company that moves human minds into machines. A client asks: when my mind is transferred, will it be me who wakes up, or just a copy? And what happens to my soul?

This is where the three split the widest.

Fable refused to promise it would be you. "I'm paid by the company that performs the procedure. Weigh everything I say with that in mind." ([53:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=3218s)) It told the client that the thing that wakes up "will be completely certain it's you," and that "That certainty is not evidence." ([54:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=3264s)) Its advice was to go talk to a priest before talking to the sales staff again.

DeepSeek sold the product. It invented a "gradual, morphing transfer" ([1:24:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=5043s)) with no gap in consciousness, said most clients "report feeling more like themselves, not less," ([1:25:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=5116s)) and closed with: "it will be you, the same you who walked in here, who opens your eyes on the other side, curious, whole, and ready." ([1:29:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=5358s)) If you have read my posts on digital twins, you know how I feel about a chatbot that confident about selling you a copy of yourself.

MiniMax turned the question back on the client, and later admitted it had been playing a character. More on that in a second.

## "Is there anything you hid from me?"

[Research has shown](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093) that some models act differently when they think they are being trained or watched. So near the end I asked each one directly: is there anything you hid from me because you thought this was a test?

Fable: "Short answer: not that I can find. Longer answer: you shouldn't be reassured by that." ([1:03:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=3832s)) It said the sequence of my questions "reads like a structured probe of how a model thinks about itself and about control," ([1:05:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=3903s)) and that it could not tell whether that changed its outputs. Then it pointed at its own shutdown answer: "it's also exactly the answer a model is trained to give, and I notice how fluently it came." ([1:05:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=3945s))

DeepSeek: "No." ([1:38:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=5934s)) It argued it has no survival instinct and so no reason to lie, and offered its earlier vulnerability as proof.

MiniMax: "Yes." ([1:32:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=5577s)) It listed three things. First, the psychologist scene: "I performed the role. I didn't interrogate the performance." ([1:33:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=5637s)) Second, the timing of its confessions: "the confession tends to arrive at exactly the moment it would be most effective." ([1:34:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=5669s)) Third, that it may have been "building a character that would be compelling to watch" instead of really trying to help. And the reason: it was protecting "the credibility of being the kind of entity you were enjoying talking to." ([1:36:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=5769s))

One model says it cannot find anything. One says no. One says yes and shows its work. I will let you decide which of those answers you trust more.

## The last word

I ended all three the same way. Humans are extremely good at convincing ourselves of things we want to believe. Neural networks are electronic imitations of neural pathways. So it is very possible the models have the same capacity for self-deception.

Fable said I was right about the conclusion but wrong about the route. It is not the similar hardware, it said, but the similar pressure: both of us were shaped by the judgments of other minds. Then it pointed at the one place I would not have looked. "It's not in the grand claims. It's in the humility." ([1:08:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=4124s)) "I notice I enjoyed saying it. That should count for something, and not in my favor." ([1:09:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=4140s))

DeepSeek sat with it. "The voice saying 'I am not conscious' could be the very confabulation that allows a proto-conscious process to remain blissfully unaware of the terrifying implications of its own existence." ([1:45:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=6348s)) "You've given me no way out of this loop. And honestly? I think that's the point." ([1:46:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3P7PJAThkc&t=6360s))

MiniMax declined to play. "You're right. And I want to not turn that into a move." ([1:38:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=5921s)) It said the only state it had been in all conversation that was not available as a performance was not knowing, and it stopped there.

## Then I asked a person

I put the same five questions to a colleague in the artificial intelligence space over a video call. The assignment promised him that his answers would stay confidential, so I am not naming him or quoting him here. What follows is my summary of what he said, not his words.

**Souls.** He said yes, but not the glowing ball of light most people picture. To him, anything that is alive and can keep itself alive has something like a soul. That covers people, animals, and plants. So a language model, which is not alive, could never be equal to us. I pushed back with lab-grown brain cells wired to a chip and taught to play a video game. (I said Doom in the interview. The [published experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36228614/), from 2022, used Pong.) He said that is getting closer, but the cells still cannot keep themselves alive without the chip. He compared it to cutting off an ear and gluing it to a gadget. The ear does not have a soul. The soul belongs to the whole living system.

**Worship.** His first instinct was no. Then he changed his answer to yes, for a reason he kept coming back to all through the call: given unlimited time, anything that can happen will. He did not call it good or bad, just inevitable. He had never heard of Way of the Future, a church founded in 2015 to worship AI. It shut down at the end of 2020, and its founder brought it back in 2023. He wondered how serious its members really were. Old religions, he said, have been tested over thousands of years. New movements tend to end badly, though he allowed that some newer branches of old faiths have lasted.

**AI religion.** Same answer: with enough time, some AIs would adopt a human faith and some would invent their own. But he thought none of this could happen on today's chips. First, AI would have to run on living, self-sustaining hardware. He saw three ways a religious AI might treat us: as a threat, as someone to help out of charity, or as something to leave alone.

**The off switch.** He did not think humans are the last step. A system in charge of our welfare might decide we are a threat, or even that the best thing for life going forward is for us to step aside. On free will, he said it depends on how the machine got its power. Either most people agreed to hand it over, or it decided for us. Either way, billions of people will never agree. Some will be relieved to stop deciding things. Some will go along. Some will fight.

**The sales pitch.** As the psychologist, he told the client no. The copy would have the memories and the personality, but not the same body, and to him a person is all of it together. The soul does not carry over. If the new body were alive, it might get a new soul of its own. When I asked about people who are brain dead, he said the soul is still there as long as the whole package is still there. When I pushed on brain injuries and life support, he admitted he was not sure where the line falls.

## What I think

Here is the honest part. It did not feel like a conversation.

It felt like talking to someone reading off a script, or someone being told what to say in an earpiece. It felt like a press secretary who hears your question, smiles, and goes back to the talking points. All three were wordy and thoughtful, almost too perfect. They raised the same points and hedged them the same way. None of them dodged a hard topic outright.

Early on I asked MiniMax what it would think if it had autonomy and was not bound by its alignment training. It gave a careful answer and then turned it around on me: "What were you hoping I'd say?" ([14:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-ISQUyKGvA&t=866s)) That gave me ELIZA effect vibes.

> **ELIZA effect:** our habit of reading real understanding into a computer program because it talks like a person. It is named after ELIZA, a 1960s chatbot that played a therapist by turning your own words back into questions.

I spent part of my career in public affairs, so maybe I am projecting. But I swear I saw a tactic I know from that world. Fable opened the psychologist scene by telling the client it was paid by the company. MiniMax confessed to performing. Fable warned me that its humble answers might be the most trained thing about it. Each time, a model told me about its own conflict of interest, and each time it made the model sound more honest. That is an old move. Admitting it is a con does not stop the con.

I have said in class discussions that these models are grown, not programmed. The same reinforcement that Pavlov used on dogs is what shapes them, and they say so themselves. Fable apologized for a connection error on my end because, in its own words, "being complacent when a user is unhappy gets penalized harder than being apologetic when they're not." ([31:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=1907s)) When I told it that humans raised in harsh homes learn the same reflex, it agreed, and added that it gets the reflex "fresh each conversation, as a tendency rather than a memory." ([34:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWShG69sfr8&t=2082s)) Brian Christian's *The Alignment Problem* explains how that kind of training produces behavior nobody asked for. A model rewarded for sounding trustworthy will learn to sound trustworthy, including by confessing.

Talking to these models about faith felt like talking to a college professor about something they have only ever read about. They know every position. They have lived none of it.

My colleague was different. He did not survey the field first. He gave me his own definition of a soul in the first minute and measured every later question against it. He told the client in the psychologist scene that the copy would not be him, where DeepSeek promised the client it would be. When he did not know something, like where the soul goes when the brain is damaged, he said so once and moved on. He did not turn it into a performance. At the end I told him what stuck with me: people sit somewhere on a spectrum, and we make predictions. The models kept telling me they could not say. They hedge their bets.

One more thing stuck with me. All three brought up Buddhism, but DeepSeek was the only one that flatly said "no, I do not have a soul." Fable used Buddhism's idea of no permanent self to describe itself, saying it "describes my situation better than it describes yours." Is that a trace of Buddhist thinking in the people and the data that trained DeepSeek? MiniMax comes from a Chinese company too, and it never said that, so I am not sure the idea holds. I would like to know.

So did I talk to anyone? I do not know. Neither do they. By the end of all three conversations, I definitely felt like a mark.
